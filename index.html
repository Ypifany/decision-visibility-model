Many of the decisions that shape an outcome happen long before the moment that looks like the decision. This page lets you check how well you can see one of yours.

How the Decision Visibility Model works

DVM does not grade a decision as right or wrong. It looks at the distance between what you expected and what reality is showing you. That distance is information, not failure. It moves in three steps, always about one decision at a time.

  1. Look back

    What were you betting on, what happened, and where is the gap?

    You can try this below.

  2. Look at now

    What do you really have, what did you hand off, and is this still the right problem?

    Done together in sessions.

  3. Look ahead

    What is your new bet, what will you watch, and what will you do?

    Done together in sessions.

The Clarity Check

Four short questions about one decision. You can pick one that is behind you, one that is still playing out, or one you have not made yet.

Question 1 of 4

Which decision do you want to look at?

Write it in a few words. It can be about work, a tool, AI, money, or your life. For example: taking a new job, or letting AI answer customer emails.

Where is this decision now?

Who or what helped you decide?

Choose all that apply.

Why did you choose it?

Most big decisions have more than one reason, and some are quiet ones. Choose all that apply.

If this started to go off track, how would you know?

    A short look back

    Three questions, done on your own. Short answers are fine. Nobody is grading this.

    Question 1 of 3

    What were you hoping this decision would do?

    Think about the situation you wanted, not the plan. If it worked out exactly the way you wanted, what would be different?

    What were you betting on?

    A bet is not an accusation. It is what you acted as though would happen. For example: "I acted as though my reviews were being checked the same way I checked them."

    What happened?

    Write up to three things. Then choose what kind of information each one is. If you are not sure, say so. Many things that happen are a fact and someone's judgment mixed together.
